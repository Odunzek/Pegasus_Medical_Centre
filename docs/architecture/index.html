How the website fits together

Four pictures that show how the clinic's website works: who changes what, where the information lives, how the pages are built from it, and what happens when a patient books. Written for a reader who prefers to see it rather than read it.

1 · The big picture

Two systems, one website

The website is made of two parts. A content back office where clinic staff type in hours, doctors, services and notices, and the public website that patients see. The website reads from the back office and rebuilds itself whenever something is published. Booking and enquiries deliberately happen outside both, so no patient information is ever stored in the website.

2 · Who changes what

Two kinds of login, no developer for everyday changes

Editor · clinic manager, front desk

Opening hours and holiday closures. The announcement bar ("Flu shots from 1 Nov"). Notices and articles. Questions and answers. The wording on doctor and service pages.

Admin · clinic owner, Kempyre

Everything an editor can, plus: the booking link, the emergency wording, the privacy contact, approving a doctor's profile before it goes live, adding a new clinic or service, and user accounts.

Developer · only for new features

A new kind of page, a new language, a new integration. Not needed for content, hours, photos or doctors. This is what the maintenance arrangement covers.

3 · Where the information lives

Seven kinds of record, each fact in one place

Everything on the site is one of seven kinds of record. The arrows show how they link. Each fact is typed once: the York clinic's hours live on the York clinic record, and every page that shows hours reads them from there.

Why this matters for growth: a Mississauga clinic is a second Location record marked "opening soon". Physiotherapy is a Service marked "planned". Both can exist in the back office months before they appear on the site, and switching them on is a status change, not a rebuild.

4 · How pages are assembled

Each page is a view of the records

No page has its own copy of the facts. The homepage, for example, shows the York clinic's "open until 6:00 p.m." line because it reads the clinic's hours and today's date; nobody types "open until 6".

6 · Decisions still open

What the clinic needs to tell us before this is final

  • Booking: which system patients book with today, and whether there is an online booking link to put behind the Book button. If there is none, the button says "Call to book".
  • Enquiries: the clinic email address the enquiry form should deliver to (a clinic inbox, not a personal one).
  • Services at launch: which of the named areas of care are live on day one, and their public names.
  • Providers: names, titles, credentials, languages and photos for the three launch profiles, each approved by the provider.
  • Languages: English and Portuguese at launch, French switched on when its pages are translated. Confirm who provides translations.
