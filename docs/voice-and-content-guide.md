# Pegasus Medical Centre: voice and content guide

The one document that says how the website speaks, in all three languages, and what it may never say. Read by whoever writes (people, the `copywriter` and `translator` agents) and whoever checks (`content-reviewer`). Facts come only from `docs/client-facts-2026-10-04.md`; this guide is about words.

## 1. Who we are talking to

Patients of every age in York, Toronto, many of them older, some unwell or worried, some reading in their second or third language, most on a phone. Nobody comes to a clinic website for fun: they want to know if they can be seen, when, where, and what to bring. Write for the person who is 74, has a sore knee, and is deciding whether to walk over now.

## 2. Voice

Warm, plain and reassuring, like a good family doctor explaining something. Confident without selling. Calm without being cold.

- Speak to the patient as **you** and about the clinic as **we** ("Our family doctors and nurses are here for every age").
- **One idea per sentence.** Short sentences. Say what happens and what to do next.
- **Plain words.** "Doctor" not "physician" in running text (the title field is where "Family physician" lives). "Walk in" not "walk-in service is available". "Bring your health card" not "please ensure you present valid documentation".
- **Specific beats general.** "Register at the desk; it takes about five minutes" beats "a simple registration process".
- **No exclamation marks, no puns, no emoji, no jargon** unless a patient would say it (OHIP, walk-in, prescription are fine).
- **Never anxious.** No "don't delay", no scare lines. Reassurance comes from clarity.

Headlines are sentence case, short, and human: "Care that takes its time." "Everyone is welcome here." "Find us in York." Not "Comprehensive Primary Care Solutions".

Things that read as machine-written and are banned: "it's not X, it's Y" reveals; "no X, no Y, no Z" triplets; sentences that trail into extra comma clauses; "seamless", "journey", "holistic", "state-of-the-art", "world-class", "elevate", "empower".

## 3. What the site may never say (Ontario rules)

The College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario (CPSO) regulates physician advertising; PHIPA governs health privacy. The website therefore:

- Carries **no testimonials, reviews, ratings, star counts or patient quotes**, anywhere, in any language, including screenshots of Google reviews.
- Makes **no superlatives or comparisons**: no "best", "leading", "top", "number one", "better than", "fastest".
- Makes **no guarantees or promises of outcomes**: no "we will cure", "guaranteed same-day", "you will feel better".
- Uses **no invented statistics**: no "10,000 patients served", "98% satisfaction" unless the clinic has the figure and approves it.
- Describes providers **factually only**: name, title, credentials (as granted), regulatory college, languages, areas of focus, locations. No "renowned", "expert", "specialist" unless it is the registered specialty. A profile is published only after the provider approves it and the approval is recorded in the CMS.
- Gives **no medical advice** beyond "what to bring" and "when to come"; nothing that tells a patient to treat something themselves.
- Says **nothing about a patient**, ever, and asks for no health information in any form. The enquiry form takes name, phone and preferred day only.
- Shows the **emergency line** wherever the clinic's address, phone or hours appear: "If you are experiencing a medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department."
- Names the clinic's **privacy contact** on the Privacy page once the clinic supplies it (PHIPA).

## 4. Facts, placeholders and drafts

- A fact is only a fact if it is in `docs/client-facts-2026-10-04.md`. Everything else is a placeholder and is written **in square brackets**: "[Typical wait: to be confirmed]", "[Dr. First Last]". Brackets are never removed by a writer; only by a fact arriving.
- Records a writer produces are **review: draft** until Dr. Khurshid (or the person he names) approves them. Production never shows a draft. Preview builds mark drafts with a Draft badge.
- Never repeat a fact that lives in a record: hours, phone and address come from the Location; do not type them into an FAQ answer or a page body. Write "during our opening hours" and let the site show the hours.
- Never invent a service, a provider, a language spoken, a parking arrangement or a fee.
- Stock photographs are stand-ins and are flagged `temporary`. Alt text describes what is in the picture plainly ("A doctor talking with an older patient"); it does not claim it is Pegasus.

## 5. Spelling, names and formats (English)

- **Canadian English**: centre, colour, organize (Oxford -ize is the Canadian norm; "immunization", "specialize"), programme → program, licence (noun) / license (verb), cheque, neighbourhood.
- The clinic is **Pegasus Medical Centre**, always with Centre, never Center, never "PMC" in patient-facing text. The former name, York Health Choice Centre, appears only where the About page or a notice explains the change ("formerly York Health Choice Centre").
- **Phone**: 416 656 7272 as displayed in the Location record (the site makes the link). Do not write "(416) 656-7272" or "+1" in copy.
- **Times**: 9:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. (lower-case a.m./p.m. with periods, "to" not a dash, never "9am"). **Days**: Monday to Friday. **Dates**: Monday, October 5 (no ordinal).
- **Addresses**: 605 Rogers Road, Unit D5, Toronto ON M6M 1B9; the neighbourhood is "York" in prose ("our clinic in York").
- **OHIP** in capitals; "health card" lower case; "walk-in" hyphenated as an adjective ("walk-in clinic") and "walk in" as a verb.
- Numbers one to nine in words, 10 and above in figures, except times, ages and prices.
- Sentence case for headings, labels and actions ("Book an appointment", not "Book An Appointment"). Spaced capitals only inside the logo.
- Actions start with a verb and say where they lead: "Book an appointment", "Call the clinic", "Get directions", "See all services".

## 6. French and Portuguese

- French is **Canadian French (fr-CA)**: "courriel", "clinique sans rendez-vous", "carte Santé" (OHIP card), "médecin de famille", "prendre rendez-vous", "24 h" time style as "9 h à 18 h" (the site formats times; copy uses the same style). Formal **vous** throughout.
- Portuguese is **European Portuguese (pt-PT)**, the variety of most Portuguese-speaking patients in this part of Toronto: "marcar consulta", "sem marcação", "cartão de saúde" (OHIP), "médico de família", "centro médico"; "você"-less formal register (third-person polite forms, "pode", "ligue-nos"). Times "9:00 às 18:00".
- Translate meaning, not words. If a sentence does not work in the language, rewrite it; keep it as short as the English.
- Keep the same facts, the same brackets and the same draft status as the English. Never add a fact in translation.
- Every French and Portuguese text written by Kempyre carries **native review pending** until a native speaker has read it; a language is enabled for the public only after that review (Site settings → Languages enabled).

## 7. Page by page: what each page is for

| Page | The patient's question | Tone notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage | Can I be seen, and how? | One headline, one action, the hours, then the ways in. Nothing clever. |
| Services index | What do you do? | Plain names a patient would say; grouped; planned services in one quiet line. |
| Service page | Is this for me, what happens, what do I bring? | Specific and uneven: say what is true of this service, not boilerplate. |
| Find us / Location | Where, when, how do I get in? | Address, hours, parking, step-free entrance, transit, the entrance photo. |
| Our doctors / profile | Who will I see, do they speak my language? | Factual only (section 3). |
| New patients | Will you take me, what happens first? | Step by step; the brackets stay until 4.5 is answered. |
| Fees and coverage | What will it cost me? | Only what the clinic approves for publication (5.6). |
| Resources | The question I was about to phone about | The front desk's real questions, in their words, short answers. |
| About | Who are you, and what happened to the old name? | The clinic's story in Dr. Khurshid's words; the rebrand explained in one sentence. |
| Contact | How do I reach you? | Phone first, then the form (name, phone, preferred day), then address and hours. |
| Privacy, Accessibility, Terms | Required pages | Plain language; the privacy contact; how to ask for help with the site. |

## 8. Checklist for anything written

Before a record moves from draft to approved, someone confirms:

1. Every fact traces to client-facts or is in square brackets.
2. Nothing from section 3 (testimonials, superlatives, guarantees, advice, patient data).
3. Centre, Canadian spelling, the time/phone/date formats above.
4. In the voice (section 2), with none of the banned phrases.
5. No fact duplicated from a record (hours, phone, address).
6. French and Portuguese match the English in meaning, brackets and draft status, and are marked for native review.
7. Emergency line present wherever clinic details appear.
8. Approved by the clinic (name and date recorded).
